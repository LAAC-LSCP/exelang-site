# SIEL

**SIEL keeps ELSI running after ExELang ends. It is a non-profit research infrastructure, hosted at ENS-PSL and CNRS, that gives laboratories around the world continued access to machine-learning tools for long-form, child-centered recordings.**

## Why SIEL?

The ExELang project (ERC Consolidator Grant, 2021–2026) built ELSI, the ExELang Legacy Support Interface. ELSI is a web platform that lets researchers organize daylong audio recordings, run state-of-the-art speech-processing models on them, and extract developmental metrics without writing code or managing GPU servers. It has been used by research teams on six continents, and its models are trained on more than 20 languages.

ERC funding ended in 2026. A tool like ELSI needs ongoing engineering, secure hosting, and computing resources, and project grants are not designed to cover those costs over the long term. SIEL is our answer: a stable, transparent structure that keeps the platform maintained, improving, and available to the research community.

**Use Case:** *"I want to keep analyzing my lab's daylong recordings with ELSI after the ExELang project ends."*

---

## What SIEL Provides

- **Continued access to ELSI.** Upload and manage standardized datasets, get automatic annotations (who is speaking, when, and to whom), and extract metrics such as adult word counts, child vocalizations, and conversational turns.
- **Maintained, up-to-date models.** This includes the Voice Type Classifier (VTC2) and other models created during ExELang, which are re-validated as new versions are released.
- **Secure, GDPR-compliant hosting** on European academic infrastructure. 
- **Cross-cultural coverage.** The models are built and evaluated on recordings from many languages and cultural settings, not only Western, English-speaking households.
- **Transparency.** The underlying tools stay open source, and the models' validation against human annotation is documented and published.

## How SIEL Works

SIEL operates on a **non-profit, cost-recovery** basis. Fees cover only the real costs of running the infrastructure: engineering staff, hosting, and computing. Revenue goes back into maintaining and improving the platform.
